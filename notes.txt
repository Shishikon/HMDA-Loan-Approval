log reg:
Logistic Regression is a linear model. If your loan dataset has complex, non-linear
interactions between features (which is common in financial risk/loan datasets),
Logistic Regression will hit a hard ceiling on performance for minority classes,
whereas tree-based models like Random Forest or XGBoost can slice the feature space
into rules to catch minority patterns much better.


The Limitation of Linear Models: Logistic Regression relies on a linear decision boundary.
When dealing with severe class imbalance on complex data, forcing a linear model to balance
classes results in an unacceptable surge of false positives (low precision).

Precision-Recall Trade-off: You can clearly demonstrate that while balancing weights improves
recall (catching the minority class), it severely damages precision for a linear classifier.

Why Non-Linear Models Are Better Here: This justifies why you are also building Random Forests and XGBoost.
Tree-based models don't draw single straight lines; they create branching, non-linear rules that can isolate
 minority classes much more cleanly without generating a massive wave of false positives.

Decision Tree:

Aggressive Over-correcting: The tree alters its split thresholds drastically to try and capture rare cases, which introduces a massive amount of false positives.

Ruining the Tree Structure: Decision trees split data recursively. When weights are forced into the impurity calculations (like Gini or Entropy), it can force the tree to grow in a suboptimal way, destroying its overall predictive power for the majority class without properly stabilizing the minority class.

Severe Accuracy Drop: Because your dataset is heavily imbalanced, the unbalanced tree achieves ~90% accuracy largely by correctly predicting the dominant majority class. When the balanced model tries to force fairness, it misclassifies a huge chunk of the majority class, dragging your overall accuracy and F1 down to 76%.
